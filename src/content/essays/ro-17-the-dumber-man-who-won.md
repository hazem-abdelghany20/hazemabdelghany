Now the version of the doctrine that actually burns. Somewhere in your phone right now is a man you privately know to be less able than you — less sharp, less disciplined, less honest about his numbers — and he is winning bigger than you. The exit, the following, the house, the ease. You minimize him out loud and track him obsessively in private (Chapter 4 told you envy is precise; this is where its precision hurts most). Every ambitious person under thirty has this man, and what you conclude about him will quietly set the temperature of your whole inner life. So let's do the analysis properly instead of at 1 a.m.

First: **your data is garbage, and not accidentally.** You are comparing your interior — the doubt, the debt, the drafts, the grind — against his *broadcast*, and the broadcast is a curated product. But the deeper corruption is statistical, and <button class="ref" data-ref="fooled-by-randomness">Nassim Taleb</button> built a career forcing people to see it: you are looking at a *survivor* and mistaking him for a sample. For every dumber man who won, there is a graveyard of equally dumber men who took the same reckless bets and vanished — and the graveyard doesn't post. Visible winners are heavily enriched for luck by the very process that made them visible; fortune selected them onto your screen, then invited you to read her choice as merit. <button class="ref" data-ref="the-success-equation">Michael Mauboussin</button> sharpened the rule: the more luck a field contains, the less its extreme outcomes tell you about skill — and startups, content, and markets are among the luckiest fields humans have built. In such fields, the scoreboard's top rows are closer to lottery results than to rankings, and studying the jackpot winner's habits is astrology with a business vocabulary.

Second — and this one requires a mirror — **check your own ledger before you cry luck.** Sometimes the dumber man isn't dumber where it counted. He shipped while you polished. He made four hundred asks while you refined the deck. He stayed in one lane for six years of Chapter 13's desert while you sampled elegantly. "Dumber" as you're using it usually means *worse at the parts I'm good at* — and the fog doesn't weight your favorite variables just because they're yours. Run the honest audit from Chapter 14: was his input actually smaller, or just differently shaped? If his volume beat your refinement, that's not luck. That's a lesson wearing an irritating face — take it, it's the cheapest tuition you'll ever get.

But suppose the audit comes back clean. His input really was smaller, his judgment really is worse, and he really did just catch the wave you missed by a month. This happens — constantly; the fog guarantees it. What then? Then you are standing at the exact fork this book exists for, and the two roads are very clear. One road: conclude the game is rigged, effort is a sucker's bet, and either quit or go bitter — the resentment horseman, mounted permanently, taxing every remaining day of your one life to pay for a stranger's luck. The other road: notice that his outcome tells you *nothing about your assignment*. Your effort was never a wager placed to beat him; there is no shared pot, no relative grading, no seat he took that was addressed to you. His rizq was written for him and yours for you, and the tradition says wanting *the thing itself* — provision like it, blessing like it — is fine and human; the disease it forbids is *hasad*, wanting it off *his* table specifically, the zero-sum reading of a non-zero-sum decree. Under that reading, comparison isn't just painful. It's a category error — auditing accounts that were never joined.

And here is the practical mechanism, because this chapter also has to survive Monday: comparison is an *attention decision*, and you've been trained since Chapter 2 to treat attention as money. Every hour inside his highlight reel is an hour withdrawn from the only ledger you can act on. So starve the mechanism. Unfollow what reliably burns — this is armory maintenance, not weakness. Convert what envy is precise about (the protocol from Chapter 9: name it, price it, extract one action or drop it). And when his name still flares in your chest — it will — finish the audit with the du'aa the tradition built for exactly this moment: ask blessing *for him*, out loud, deliberately. Not because it's polite. Because it is the one move that makes his win and your heart compatible — envy cannot survive sustained blessing; the two circuits won't run at once. Try it before you doubt it.

The dumber man who won is not evidence about you. He was never evidence about you. Close the tab. Your ledger's open.
