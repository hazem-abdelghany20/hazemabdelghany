I was listening to an Ahmad Al-Shugairi episode about a single verse: *wa yarzuqhu min haythu lā yahtasib*, "and He provides for him from where he never expected." Eight minutes. I came out of it with an idea I want to work by for the rest of my life.

## All the calculating is ours

We calculate. We make plans, budgets, forecasts. My whole job is numbers and plans, so I'm the first one calculating. And that's asked of us.

But the verse says provision comes *from where he never expected*. After all that calculating, it arrives from somewhere else entirely.

Al-Shugairi's example was Steve Jobs. He had ambition and dreams, but however much he calculated, there is no way he calculated that what he started at twenty-one would become the biggest company in the world.

## Written in the sky

> Wa fī al-samā'i rizqukum wa mā tū'adūn. And in the sky is your provision, and what you are promised.

God didn't say in the earth. He said in the sky. Your provision is written before you are born, exactly, how much you will get in your life.

The first time I heard it, I felt a strange calm. Then the obvious question: if it's written, does the work not matter?

It does. And here is a line from Sheikh al-Sha'rawi that Al-Shugairi quoted, and it opened the whole thing up for me:

**The amount is written. You choose where you take it from.** Halal or haram. The provision reaches you either way. The whole difference is the road.

So it matters a great deal, just not the way I thought. I'm not running to raise the number. The number is already written. I'm running to reach it through the right door.

## The hotel manager

Al-Shugairi says he heard this story from the man himself.

A man was managing a hotel in Egypt. The hotel sold alcohol, and he wasn't at peace with his work. He went to Sheikh al-Sha'rawi and told him. The sheikh said: leave the job.

He said: but I have a family, I have responsibilities.

The sheikh recited: "Whoever is mindful of God, He makes a way out for him, and provides for him from where he never expected."

The man went back to his office and sat down to write his resignation. While he was writing it, the phone rang. The regional director: we want to promote you, to run the hotel in Madinah.

**While he was writing the resignation.**

The provision was written. Same profession, same experience. What changed is that he chose to be mindful of God, and God led him to his provision through a halal door. From a place he would never have calculated.

## Provision isn't only money

The first law Al-Shugairi gave: provision is wider than money. Health is provision. A clear mind is provision. Children are provision. Peace of mind is provision.

He told of an old actor who, before he was famous, was poor, and prayed: Lord, a million pounds, even with cancer. He did become a millionaire, and he did get cancer, and the millions did him no good.

So the poor man whose mind is at rest may have more provision than the rich man in front of him.

## Gratitude is the source

Then the part I took with me more than anything. Al-Shugairi said provision has secrets that increase it, and the first is gratitude:

> La'in shakartum la-azīdannakum. If you are grateful, I will surely give you more.

He called it a law of the universe, like gravity. Hold a pen and let go, it falls. Every time, since Adam. Be grateful, it increases. Every time.

That flipped the order in my head. I used to see gratitude as a reaction: God gives, I thank. The verse says the opposite. **Gratitude isn't the end of the chain, it's the start.** It is the source the increase comes out of.

The other secrets he named: keeping ties with family, "whoever wants his provision widened and his life lengthened, let him keep ties with his kin." And doing the work well, "God loves that when one of you does a job, he perfects it."

The good part is that all three are in my hands every day. Be grateful. Call my family. Do the thing in front of me properly.

## In the hand, not in the heart

Al-Shugairi was clear this isn't a message against money. The strong believer is better than the weak one, and money is strength. Companions like Uthman ibn Affan and Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf were among the richest people of their time.

But Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf left all his wealth in Makkah and emigrated. When his brother in Madinah offered him half of everything he had, he said: *no, just show me the way to the market.* And he became one of the richest men in Madinah again.

The money was in his hand. Not in his heart.

## What I'm working by

- **The amount is written.** I won't worry about it.
- **The road is a choice.** I'll choose halal, even when it looks more expensive.
- **Gratitude is the beginning.** Not the reward at the end.

The rest comes from where I never expected.
