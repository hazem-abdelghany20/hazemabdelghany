It is a Sunday at the end of August, in Cairo, in the year 2026, and I am writing this on the first morning of a sixty-day commitment I have made to my own drill — deep work timed daily, the five prayers whole, the mind fed on real pages instead of the feed. Day one of sixty. Streak: zero. Everything in this book, about to be tested again by its own author, who has broken every rule in it before and expects to break some again.

I tell you this so you know what kind of book you just read. Not a summit report — I am not writing from the far side of success, tanned and certain. This is a field manual, written *from the field*, by a man mid-campaign: businesses that might not work, a knee with hardware in it, a country that doesn't hand out arenas, a faith I have to re-choose every dawn, and a phone in the other room right now specifically because I do not trust myself with it. Whatever authority these pages have comes from one source only: I need every chapter of them, still, today.

So let me spend the last page the way the knight would.

You have the kit — you always did: the body, the attention, the horse, the gauges, the silence, and the strange light on the panel that comes on when they align. You know the drill — the blade held, the day as the unit, the horsemen met, the forge, the code. You have a campaign to choose and clocks to keep and company to ride with. And you know, now, the two sentences the whole thing stands on. *Fair at the input, unfair at the output — live at the input.* And: *tie the camel; kneel; then ride.*

Somewhere ahead of you, in the fog, your results are already waiting — the calls and the silences, the wins that will feel smaller than promised and the losses that will teach you your next book. I don't know which are yours, and neither do you, and this morning that is finally, fully, none of our business. The gates are open. The fog is thick. The effort was already the reward — not as consolation, but as accounting.

Yalla. We ride.
